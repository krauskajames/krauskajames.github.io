# Opening story (chapter 1)

Written by James Krauska; approved November 24, 2026. Use as the opening of the page, unchanged.

For generations my family has been coming to Lake Winnebago in the summer. Every year my extended family would stay at an uncle's cottage on the lake's east shore for nine days. Many sunny days that were perfect for swimming were cut short when mats of algae blew in from the other side of the lake. Swirling, marbled blues and greens formed a thick mat on the surface that smelled like sulfur. Swimming, kayaking and fishing were canceled. It was board game time for the rest of the week.

Those mats have been engraved in my mind for years. They were beautiful, and they stank. My grandma JoAnn always said, "The northwest wind blows the blue-green algae from the other side of the lake." But we were there for only nine days a year, which is a worm's-eye view of the problem. I wanted to check for myself whether she was right. So I went digging...
