North Point Water Tower, in the round

An interactive 3D model of Milwaukee's North Point Water Tower, built from drone video. Turn it, zoom in on the lantern, and look at the stonework from any side.

Rendered live in your browser with three.js and Spark
What it is

A Gaussian splat stores a scene as millions of tiny, soft, coloured blobs instead of a mesh of triangles. Together they reproduce what the camera saw, including fine detail like carved stone, slate roofs and the copper lantern, that a traditional photogrammetry mesh tends to smooth over.

I'm building this workflow at PX Design, where drone splats of client buildings will sit alongside site plans so building managers can inspect a property from their desk.

How it was made
  1. Fly. Drone video in orbits around the tower with exposure locked, so every frame shares the same brightness.
  2. Check the logs. Read the drone's per-frame flight logs (.SRT) to confirm the orbits close and the heights differ before processing anything.
  3. Extract frames. Pull a few hundred stills from the video with ffmpeg, scaled to 1920 px, and drop blurry ones.
  4. Solve the cameras. COLMAP matches features between frames and works out where the camera was for each one, producing a sparse point cloud.
  5. Train the splat. A Gaussian splatting trainer fits the blobs to the frames from those camera positions.
  6. Clean and publish. Crop the scene and remove floaters in SuperSplat, export a compressed file, and render it here with Spark.

Problems worth solving

  • Ghost copies. Repetitive features like rows of identical windows can fool the camera solve into placing a second, smaller copy of a building. Stricter registration thresholds and matching settings fixed it.
  • Coverage, not frame count, decides quality. Several orbits at different heights beat one long pass. I now check the flight logs before processing to catch gaps early.
  • Web delivery. The raw splat is hundreds of megabytes. The compressed version loads in seconds and runs on a phone.
My role

I planned and flew the capture, ran the processing pipeline, cleaned the model, and built this viewer page.
