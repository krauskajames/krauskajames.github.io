Pollinator Habitat Explorer

Where in Wisconsin can the host plants of three specialist pollinators grow? I modeled statewide habitat suitability for swamp milkweed, wild lupine and fringed loosestrife from iNaturalist records, WorldClim climate, SoilGrids soils and the DNR's WISCLAND 2.0 land cover.

Method
  1. Occurrences. 454,717 research-grade iNaturalist plant observations in Wisconsin (GBIF). Kept records with location uncertainty ≤ 1 km, then thinned to one record per ~1 km cell to reduce clustering: 626 swamp milkweed, 411 wild lupine, 232 fringed loosestrife.
  2. Predictors. 19 WorldClim 2.1 bioclimatic variables and elevation (30 arc-seconds), 13 SoilGrids 2.0 soil properties converted to real units, and 8 percent-cover layers from WISCLAND 2.0 Level 2, all resampled to one 0.0025° grid and masked to Wisconsin.
  3. Collinearity screen. Dropped any variable correlated at |r| ≥ 0.7 with a higher-priority one, leaving 22 of 41 predictors.
  4. Sampling-bias correction. A target-group bias surface — the smoothed density of all 264,899 well-located plant observations — so background points mirror where people go looking, not just where plants grow.
  5. MaxEnt 3.4.4. Auto features, regularization 1, 10,000 background points, 25% random test split, fixed seed, up to 5,000 iterations, jackknife and response curves on.
  6. Web. Outputs reprojected to Web Mercator and stored as 8-bit grayscale PNGs, coloured in the browser so every click reads the real model value.

Reading the results honestly

  • Lower AUCs than a typical student model, on purpose. Correcting for observer bias removes the easy win of “predict near Madison and Milwaukee,” so AUCs fall. The first, uncorrected version of this project reported 0.81–0.93; the rebuilt models are the more trustworthy ones.
  • Wild lupine is the strongest model (test AUC 0.84). Its predicted range traces the Central Sands and northwest sand barrens, where Wisconsin's Karner blue populations are concentrated.
  • The wetland plants needed land cover. With climate and soil alone, milkweed and loosestrife scored 0.58 and 0.56. Adding WISCLAND wetland and land-use classes raised them to 0.71 and 0.68.
  • Suitability is not presence. The maps show where conditions resemble known sites. Fine-scale habitat such as roadside ditches and stream banks sits below the 250 m grid.
  • Some residual bias remains. Fringed loosestrife's positive link with developed land likely reflects where people walk, not the plant's preference.
My role

I designed and ran this as my senior research project at UW–Stevens Point, then rebuilt it in 2026: rebuilt the predictor stack at finer resolution, fixed NoData handling, added land cover and bias correction, and published it as an interactive map.

Data preparation and the web build were written with AI-assisted coding (Claude); I chose the methods, ran the models and checked every output.

Scripts and a step-by-step reproduction guide are on GitHub.
