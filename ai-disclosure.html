AI Use Disclosure

Author
James Krauska, Geospatial & CAD Technician
Date
November 9, 2026
Subject
How I use AI in my GIS work and on this portfolio

01How this was written

I wrote the first draft of this statement by hand, outside on a bench in a park near my apartment. I drafted, wrote and typed it without the use of AI in about three hours. I did this to showcase my natural writing and to disclose my relationship with AI in an honest and professional way, where my ideas come across and not an AI chatbot’s. Afterward, I revised it using editing suggestions from Claude, an AI assistant, for length, tone and grammar.

02My view of AI

AI is a polarizing issue among working-age people, and many believe it threatens jobs across several industries. AI chatbots started coming out around the time my college experience began, and I have watched the technology grow and evolve since. I have wrestled with the threat it poses, but since I started building my portfolio website, I have come to see the role of an entry-level employee shifting toward that of a manager: someone who directs the tools, checks their work and takes responsibility for the result.

I have seen many products (e.g., websites, fliers and apps) made from quick, uncreative prompts, glanced at briefly and approved without deeper consideration. I don’t think that uses AI to its highest potential. Chatbots and agents need to be supervised and guided through their work with tact and deliberation.

03How I work with AI

I have used Claude, Anthropic’s AI assistant, since I started putting this portfolio together from revamped college labs and new projects. I don’t hand it a single prompt and publish what comes back; that hands-off approach creates bugs and errors that can’t be used in a professional capacity. Instead, we work as a team through a back-and-forth process:

  1. I write informatively and exhaustively during pre-planning and planning.
  2. I read the AI’s responses closely and ask in-depth, investigative questions that move the project forward.
  3. The AI writes hands-on labs that teach me how to get the outcome I want, and I run the steps myself.
  4. We give each other feedback.
  5. I thoroughly check and beta-test every professional deliverable before it is published.

04What AI does on this site

Claude writes most of the code on this site and in tools like DuctBuilder. I am still building my own fluency in programming languages, so I read, test and debug that code rather than writing it from scratch. The project ideas, data choices, analysis design, field work and final calls are mine, and I take responsibility for the accuracy of everything I publish. Every page on this site ends with a “Built with” line that says how it was made.

05What I bring

I believe the most important thing for an entry-level geospatial professional to learn is how the ecosystem of GIS programs can work in tandem: each program’s breadth and limits, and how each can be implemented in a project so that the wants and needs of the end user are not just met but exceeded. That is the work I want to bring to your team.
